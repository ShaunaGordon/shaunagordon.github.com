A couple years ago, I was tasked with finding the most viable game engine for us to use at Genome Studios. It was shortly after the Unity licensing kerfluffle, and between it and the Unreal Engine stuff at the time, we wanted something more sustainable. So, I evaluated a number of engines, including some oddball ones. That's how we found out about [O3DE](https://o3de.org), and though it was rough around the edges at the time, we couldn't shake the feeling of potential we saw in it.

As we got to working with it and putting it through its paces, we found exactly where those rough edges were. Being hip-deep in the community, we were also seeing where others were running into the rough edges. And we set out to smooth them.

## Enter Opal

At the beginning of the year, I started on the task of making a better project and asset manager for O3DE. It needed to not only manage projects, but engines and gems, too. It needed to be easy, it needed to remove the friction of getting started, and it needed to support multiple engines side by side.

Nine months later, [we've launched Opal](https://opallauncher.com/) to do exactly that.
